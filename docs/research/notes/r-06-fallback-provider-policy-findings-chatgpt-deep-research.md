# Fallback Provider Policy for Direct Mistral Summarisation

## Recommendation

I treated the architecture facts in your brief as the fixed baseline and checked them against Mistral’s current public legal, privacy, security, and help-centre materials dated March and April 2026. On that basis, the right decision is a **conditional go for a tightly managed PoC**, and a **clear no-go for any general self-serve or “drop in your own key” fallback**. The reason is not model capability. It is that the provider’s acceptable privacy posture depends on account-level and support-approved settings that the application cannot itself verify, while the default API posture is still retention-bearing. Mistral’s privacy policy says ordinary API input/output is retained for the time needed to generate output and then for **30 rolling days** for abuse monitoring unless Zero Data Retention is activated; its ZDR article says activation is **request-based, discretionary, and support-reviewed**; and its commercial terms still contain narrower exceptions than the simple help-centre summary “paid APIs are not used for training” suggests. citeturn7view4turn7view0turn18view1turn5view1

In practical terms, direct fallback may ship **only** as an **operator-managed, disabled-by-default, text-only summarisation path** on an organisation-controlled Mistral AI Studio setup that is under the commercial terms, has written proof of ZDR approval for the specific account/workspace, and is disclosed honestly to the user as a remote provider path with residual contractual dependencies. If any of those conditions is missing, the correct answer is **do not send the transcript remotely**. citeturn6view3turn30view0turn19view2turn27view0

## What the official materials actually establish

For business use, Mistral’s privacy policy says that when its products are used to process personal data in the context of business activities, **the customer is the controller and Mistral is the processor**. The commercial terms then say that if the customer uses the products on Mistral infrastructure, the published DPA applies. Mistral is incorporated in entity["country","France","country"], and the current docs say the API is served from entity["organization","European Union","supranational union"] data centres by default. That is a materially better starting point for a sovereignty-first product than a consumer-grade or non-EU-by-default offering, but it does not, by itself, prove the exact retention or routing posture of a particular account. citeturn6view3turn30view0turn26view0turn7view1

On retention, the public picture is precise enough to rule out any default-retention remote fallback. The privacy policy says that, for APIs, Mistral keeps input and output for the time necessary to generate the output and then for **30 rolling days to monitor abuse**, unless ZDR is activated. It then lists longer retention for other categories such as technical connection data, account data, invoices, contracts, privacy requests, and technical support records. That means even a ZDR-approved path should **not** be described as “nothing is retained”: content retention and metadata/account/support retention are separate things. The same privacy policy also says some APIs have different retention patterns altogether, including the Agents API, which keeps input and output until account termination, and the Fine-Tuning API, which keeps training data until deletion or account termination. The docs separately say uploaded files are retained for 30 days unless deleted earlier. citeturn7view4turn5view3turn26view0

On training, the official materials are directionally positive but not as clean as the simplified summaries imply. The help centre says that Scale-plan Studio/API data is not used to train models by default, while Experiment-plan API data may be used for training unless the admin opts out via the Admin Console. But the commercial terms are more exact: Mistral says it will not use customer data or outputs to train its models **except** in several carve-outs, including feedback, content flagged through automated moderation or user reporting, terms provided in an order form, and Labs models. Labs models are explicitly singled out again: the terms say they may use Labs customer data and outputs for training unless ZDR is activated, and that normal opt-out preferences do not apply to Labs. The product conclusion is that “paid API” is **necessary but not sufficient**; the contractual carve-outs still matter. citeturn5view1turn5view2turn18view1turn30view0

On routing and jurisdiction, the posture is “EU by default, but not automatically EU-only for every feature”. Mistral’s help centre says data is hosted in the EU by default, that a **US API endpoint** can be explicitly used instead, and that some features may temporarily transfer data outside the EU to the locations listed in the Trust Center subprocessor register. It also says Enterprise customers can deactivate some features that involve such transfers at organisation level. The DPA says customers may subscribe to subprocessor-change notices, may object on data-protection grounds within ten days, and may request documents and, in limited circumstances, on-site audits. For non-Americas/non-APAC commercial customers, the commercial terms use French law and French courts; the DPA’s SCC module for restricted-country transfers also points to French law and French courts. Those are meaningful enforceability signals, but they remain **contractual/operator controls**, not runtime signals the application can test. citeturn7view1turn19view0turn19view1turn30view0

Security credentials are respectable but secondary to the present decision. Mistral says it complies with SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001/27701, offers Trust Center materials, encrypts data at rest with AES-256 and in transit with TLS 1.2+, and safeguards data with encrypted backups replicated across multiple EU zones. Those controls support a baseline of seriousness, but they do **not** answer the decisive questions here: content retention, ZDR scope, and what the application can or cannot verify. citeturn19view3turn9search0turn5view7

## Exact allow and deny conditions

Direct Mistral fallback is acceptable for the PoC **only if every condition below is true**.

- The account is an **organisation-controlled commercial** Mistral AI Studio setup used in business activities, so the processor/DPA posture applies. A casual personal or consumer account is out of policy scope for this fallback. citeturn6view3turn30view0

- The Studio subscription is **Scale or stronger commercial coverage**, not Experiment. Mistral’s own help says the free API tier is for evaluation/prototyping and that actual projects and production workloads should upgrade to Scale; public training guidance also says Experiment-plan API data may be used for training unless opted out, whereas Scale-plan data is not used for training by default. citeturn29view0turn5view1turn5view2

- **Zero Data Retention is approved in writing for the specific account/workspace** used by the fallback. The public docs describe ZDR as a support-reviewed request that may be approved or denied at Mistral’s discretion; they do not describe it as a self-service or API-queryable flag. citeturn7view0turn27view3

- The fallback uses a **dedicated workspace and a dedicated workspace-scoped API key** for mena, with expiry and rotation. Workspaces are isolated environments, keys are scoped to a workspace, and usage is tracked separately. citeturn27view0turn27view2

- The application uses the **direct Mistral API on the default EU-served path only**, and never an explicit US endpoint. Because the current docs also warn that some features may transfer data outside the EU, the fallback should stay on the narrowest path possible. citeturn26view0turn7view1

- The fallback is **plain text chat-completion only**. It must not use audio transcription, file uploads, OCR, Agents, Fine-Tuning, Batch, Connectors, Libraries, Web Search, or any other feature with broader or distinct retention behaviour. The privacy policy and docs already show materially different retention for Agents, Fine-Tuning, files, and batch artefacts. citeturn7view4turn26view0

- The model must be from a **small, explicit allow-list of standard non-Labs text models**. Any model ID with the `labs` prefix should be rejected automatically. Labs have separate training rules in the commercial terms. citeturn18view1turn26view0

- The workflow must exclude **provider feedback/reporting paths** in normal use. Mistral’s commercial terms allow use of customer data and outputs associated with feedback, and the training carve-outs also include content flagged through automated moderation or user reporting. Because the public docs do not explain how ZDR interacts with those carve-outs for ordinary chat-completion traffic, the minimum acceptable Menna policy should require **written clarification from Mistral** for the specific workspace if you want to rely on a clean “no training of fallback content” statement. Without that written clarification, fallback remains a PoC-only path with a documented contractual caveat. citeturn18view1turn7view3

- If remote prompts may contain **special-category or similarly regulated personal data**, do not rely on the standard public paperwork without legal review. The published DPA schedule currently states “Special categories of personal data: None”, which is not a safe assumption for arbitrary meeting transcripts. Either keep such content out of remote fallback or obtain legal sign-off and, if needed, tailored contractual language. citeturn19view2

If **any** condition above is missing, the decision is **deny remote fallback** and stay local-only. That includes the common case where a key exists but the application has no documentary proof of plan, ZDR status, workspace provenance, or contractual scope. citeturn7view0turn27view3turn27view0

## What the application can verify and what it cannot

The application can realistically verify only a narrow technical perimeter. It can verify that it is calling the direct Mistral API host configured by the app rather than a broker, that it is sending **text only** and not audio or files, that the request is going through the chat-completions path rather than broader APIs, that the chosen model is on an internal allow-list and does not begin with `labs`, and that the key is syntactically valid and capable of making a request. It can also enforce a dedicated local configuration profile so that remote summarisation cannot happen silently. citeturn25search0turn26view0turn18view1

The application **cannot** verify the decisive privacy predicates from the API alone. The current public materials place those controls in the Admin Console, support workflow, contracts, DPA, and Trust Center: plan type, Experiment opt-out state, ZDR approval, subprocessor roster, whether an order form overrides baseline terms, whether temporary extra-EU transfers are disabled for the relevant account, and whether any enterprise-only observability feature such as Data Capture is enabled or storing more than the product expects. The app also cannot verify the provider’s actual retention execution, support-side handling, or moderation-side carve-outs. Those must be treated as **documentation, operator setup, and explicit attestation**, not as technical guarantees. citeturn5view2turn7view0turn27view3turn19view0turn30view0

## Required product behaviour for unknown or non-compliant states

When credentials exist but policy status is unknown, the correct behaviour is **not** “best effort remote fallback”. It is **remote fallback unavailable**. The product should show a crisp reason such as: the key is present, but the provider compliance status required by mena is not verified. That keeps the architecture aligned with the fact that ZDR and plan/privacy state are documented account controls rather than API-visible truth. citeturn7view0turn5view2turn27view3

If the operator has supplied the necessary documentary evidence, the product should still keep the path **disabled by default** and make it an **explicit user action**, not a silent background fallback. The disclosure should say, in plain terms, that only the text sent for summarisation leaves the device; audio, transcription, and diarisation remain local; the remote path depends on the operator’s compliant Mistral account configuration; and the application cannot itself prove provider-side contractual settings. That user-facing statement is important because the public materials leave real gaps between what the app can test and what it can only rely on contractually. citeturn7view4turn6view3turn19view1

Any change that could invalidate the attested state should flip the product back to **unverified** until re-reviewed: new key, new workspace, subscription change, provider policy update, subprocessor change, or expiry of the local compliance attestation. Mistral’s commercial terms allow updates to the terms, and the DPA/Trust Center framework contemplates evolving subprocessors and security measures. A privacy-first product should therefore treat compliance status as time-bounded, not permanent. citeturn30view0turn19view0turn19view2

For the “personal experimentation” question, the architecture should **prohibit it inside the product**. If an individual wants to run ad hoc experiments with a personal account, that can exist outside the shipped product and outside the supported architecture, but it should not be presented as a supported toggle, hidden setting, or tolerated “advanced” path within mena itself. Inside the product, that would collapse the distinction between verified policy state and user guesswork, which is exactly the policy drift you are trying to avoid. The fact that Mistral’s own controls for training and ZDR live outside the API makes that prohibition the cleaner choice. citeturn5view2turn7view0turn27view3

## Documentation requirements and residual risk

The minimum documentation bundle should include: the specific Mistral workspace and key owner; proof of Scale/commercial status; written ZDR approval for that exact workspace or account; the approved model allow-list; the statement that only chat-completion text is in scope; a note that direct Mistral API only is allowed and the US endpoint is prohibited; the review date of the provider’s terms, privacy policy, DPA, and Trust Center materials; and the named owner responsible for monitoring subprocessor and legal-term changes. Under the DPA, customers can subscribe to subprocessor updates, may object on data-protection grounds, and may request audit materials; those controls should be part of the operator runbook, not left implicit. citeturn19view0turn19view1turn30view0

The residual risks that remain even after a compliant setup are material and should be written down, not hand-waved away. First, ZDR does **not** mean zero metadata retention: technical logs, account data, invoices, and support records still have their own retention periods. Secondly, the public legal terms still contain training and handling carve-outs around feedback, automated moderation or reporting, Labs models, and order-form overrides. Thirdly, the provider’s routing posture is EU-default rather than absolutely EU-exclusive, because some features may involve temporary transfers outside the EU. Fourthly, the published DPA schedule is not obviously tailored for special-category meeting content. Fifthly, public materials reference observability features such as Data Capture, but the public pages I found do not fully specify their payload scope or how they interact with ZDR, so they should be treated as an unresolved risk surface until separately validated. citeturn7view4turn18view1turn7view1turn19view2turn22search1turn27view3

Taken together, those residuals do **not** force a blanket rejection of direct Mistral for a managed PoC. They do, however, justify a firm boundary: **operator-managed commercial workspace with written ZDR evidence and explicit product disclosure = go; everything else = no-go**. That boundary is concrete, enforceable enough for a PoC, and honest about what the application can verify versus what remains contractual or attested. citeturn7view0turn27view0turn19view0turn30view0
